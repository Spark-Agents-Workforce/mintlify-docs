From zero to your first test sale:

- **Outcome:** a complete subscription machine, in accounts you own, proven with one test sale on a store that doesn't matter.
- **Time:** about an hour, most of it watching the installer work.
- **You'll touch:**
  - The Shopify Dev Dashboard (your app and your dev store)
  - Supabase and Vercel (two Pro signups)
  - The OpenSubs installer (Launchpad)
  - Your dev store's admin and theme editor
  - Your dev storefront, once, as the test subscriber

Self-hosting sounds like a weekend of DevOps. It's an hour, and almost none of it is yours.

How the money gets made: a shopper picks **Subscription** on your product page and checks out, Shopify writes the subscription contract, and OpenSubs mirrors it into your admin and your subscriber's portal. Everything else exists to support that sale, and every piece sits in an account you own:

- **Supabase** holds your database: your subscription state, in Postgres you can open.
- **Vercel** runs your app.
- **Shopify** holds the custom app you create, and the cards: they stay in Shopify Payments and never touch OpenSubs. OpenSubs only tells Shopify when to bill.

The stack starts around \$45 a month, and nobody takes a percentage. The installer (Launchpad) assembles everything: it asks for what it needs up front, builds unattended, proves each piece, and purges its own secrets at the finish. It keeps nothing.

Today it runs against a dev store: free, disposable, zero subscribers. Watch it sell once where mistakes are free. Going live is [the next page](/platform-v2/live-for-new-orders/what-today-changes), the easy one.
