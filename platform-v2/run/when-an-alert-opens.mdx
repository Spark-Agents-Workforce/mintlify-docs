Every alert lands on one screen: **Settings > Operational health**. The watchdog checks every 15 minutes, opens an alert when a check fails, and resolves it by itself when the check passes again: a vanished alert is a healed problem. Home's **Needs attention** banner counts the open ones.

The kinds, in plain words. The numbered outages each get a fix step below:

| Alert | What it usually means |
| --- | --- |
| **Job stalled** | A scheduled job hasn't finished a run on time. If the stalled job is **Notification sender**, the app itself is usually unreachable (outage 2); if every daily job stalls at once, suspect a paused database (outage 1). |
| **Job failed** | The job's latest run errored, and the alert carries the error text. Clears on the next clean run. |
| **Queue lagging** | Queued work (emails, imports, bulk runs, Shopify updates) is piling up faster than it drains. Usually the app is unreachable (outage 2). |
| **Notification deliveries failed** | Emails or texts gave up after their retries in the last 24 hours. The fixes live on [When emails don't arrive](/platform-v2/run/when-emails-dont-arrive) and [When texts don't arrive](/platform-v2/run/when-texts-dont-arrive). |
| **Store import failed** | The initial sync of your store's data (products, customers, subscriptions) stopped partway. Home shows the same failure with a **Retry import** button; the import resumes where it stopped. |
| **Bulk operation failed** | A bulk run stopped and gave up partway. Different from finishing with a few failed rows, which never alerts. Open the run and read its results ([Change many at once](/platform-v2/run/change-many-at-once)). |
| **Promo revert abandoned** | A promotion's temporary discount is stuck on a subscription: \~24 hours of retries couldn't take it off. The promotion's own page shows the same problem. |
| `shopify-jobs-failed` | A queued Shopify update gave up after its retries. Worth reading closely: if the failed update was removing a one-time item, that item bills again next cycle. This one shows its raw name. |

<Steps>
  <Step title="Read it, then acknowledge it">
    - Each alert names its kind and the job behind it, plus when it was first seen, when the watchdog last confirmed it, and a detail line of counts or error text.
    - Click **Acknowledge** once you've read it. The alert softens and gains an **Acknowledged** badge.
    - Acknowledging is bookkeeping, not a fix: the alert stays until its check actually passes.

    <Frame>
      <img src="/images/platform-v2/run/19-open-alert.png" alt="Operational health: an open alert naming its kind and job, already acknowledged." width="640" />
    </Frame>

    <Check>
    **Checkpoint:** alert read, acknowledged, and matched to its row in the table.
    </Check>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Outage 1: a paused Supabase project">
    - Free-tier Supabase projects pause themselves, and everything scheduled stops with them, billing included. The symptom: **Job stalled** across the daily jobs.
    - The fix: unpause the project in Supabase, then watch the **Scheduled jobs** panel for runs landing again. Keep your Supabase organization on Pro so it can't pause itself again.
    - Billing catches up on its own: the next hourly tick past your billing hour sweeps everything that came due while the project slept.

    <Note>
    **Catch-up can't double-charge.** Each due cycle bills exactly once, even when catch-up lands more than one on the same day: every charge checks a per-subscription ledger that only moves forward, and repeats are refused.
    </Note>

    <Check>
    **Checkpoint:** jobs green again, alerts resolving on their own.
    </Check>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Outage 2: the app unreachable">
    - When your app's Vercel deployment goes down, the portal goes dark and queued work backs up. **Job stalled** opens for **Notification sender** within 30 minutes; **Queue lagging** follows.
    - Billing is unaffected: it runs inside your database, not the app.
    - The fix: open your Vercel dashboard and check the latest deployment. Once the app answers again, the queues drain themselves. No replay, no cleanup.

    <Check>
    **Checkpoint:** app back, queues empty, alerts resolved on their own.
    </Check>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Outage 3: a 503 after an update">
    - A 503 that says **Database schema out of date** means new code deployed before its database migrations ran. The refusal is deliberate: code against an old schema corrupts data quietly, so the platform fails loudly instead.
    - The fix is the migrate step of [Update the platform](/platform-v2/run/update-the-platform): run `npm run merchant:migrate`, then reload the site.

    <Check>
    **Checkpoint:** the site answers again. Nothing to roll back.
    </Check>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the webhook delivery panel">
    - Still on **Operational health**, one more panel worth a look. **Webhook delivery (24h)** counts what Shopify told your platform in the last day, topic by topic; **Recent failures (7d)** lists the latest failures with their error text.
    - The occasional failed row heals itself: Shopify retries failed deliveries on its own, and the retry processes as if it were the first try.
    - Sustained failures across many topics are the real signal. Check your Supabase project's Edge Function logs for the error text, then hand what you find to whoever runs your code.

    <Frame>
      <img src="/images/platform-v2/run/20-webhook-delivery.png" alt="Webhook delivery over 24 hours, counted by topic." width="640" />
    </Frame>

    <Check>
    **Checkpoint:** failed counts falling, not climbing.
    </Check>
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
**The stop rule.** A migration failing the same way twice, a 503 that stays after the migrate step of [Update the platform](/platform-v2/run/update-the-platform) reports `0 pending`, or any advice that starts with "just edit the database": stop and hand it to whoever runs your code. Never hand-edit the database.
</Warning>
