When a subscriber cancels in their portal, your cancellation flow gets the last word: a reason, then an offer matched to that reason, then, only then, the cancel. This is the feature the whole portal-first doctrine leans on ([When a subscriber asks for help](/platform-v2/run/when-a-subscriber-asks)), and it's why an admin cancel is the expensive kind.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create the flow">
    - Open **Settings > Cancellation flows** and create a flow. The builder walks four stages: name it, pick the reasons, attach the offers, review and publish.
    - Reasons are the choices a cancelling subscriber picks from ("Too expensive", "Too much product", yours to write). Their order on screen is randomized on purpose, so there's no reorder control and no first-position bias in your reason data.

    <Check>
    **Checkpoint:** every reason a real subscriber might have is on the list, in their words.
    </Check>

    <Frame>
      <img src="/images/platform-v2/run/30-flow-builder.png" alt="The cancellation flow builder: the flow's reasons with their offers, stage by stage." width="560" />
    </Frame>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Attach the offers">
    - Each reason can carry its own offer branch; the first matching branch wins, and a default offer catches everyone else.
    - Offers are the saves: a discount, a pause, a delay, a frequency change. Match them to the reason: "too expensive" wants a discount; "too much product" wants a delay or a lower frequency.
    - Follow-up offers give you a second swing after a declined first offer.

    <Check>
    **Checkpoint:** every reason lands on an offer that actually answers it, with the default as the safety net.
    </Check>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Publish, and know the switch">
    - **Save draft** keeps it private; **Publish flow** makes it live after the readiness checklist passes.
    - There is no separate on/off switch for the feature: publishing a flow turns the save machinery on, and deactivating your last flow turns it off.
    - One flow is the active default per store; targeted flows can sit alongside it.

    <Check>
    **Checkpoint:** the flow reads published, and a test cancel in the portal walks through it.
    </Check>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Tune it from the numbers">
    - **Analytics > Cancellations** shows the funnel: flow started, reason selected, offer shown, offer declined, cancelled. A reason with many starts and no saves is an offer problem; a reason nobody picks is a wording problem.
    - Offers that don't fire are usually gating, not the flow: [When a save offer doesn't show](/platform-v2/run/when-a-save-offer-doesnt-show).

    <Check>
    **Checkpoint:** you know your save rate, and which reason earns its keep.
    </Check>
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
**Only the portal runs the flow.** A cancel done in your admin skips every offer, which is exactly why the fastest reply to "please cancel me" is a login link, not a favor.
</Note>
