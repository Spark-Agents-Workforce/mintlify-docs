<PlatformTag platform="opensubs" />

<span className="os-chip">5 min</span>

Find it once; everything after happens on one screen. Schedule and quantity edits collect in the save bar; everything else takes effect the moment you confirm. The map, then the steps:

| To change | Go to |
| --- | --- |
| Next billing date, frequency | the **Schedule** card |
| Products, quantities, swaps | the **Items** card |
| Delivery address | the pencil on the **Customer** card |
| Shipping price, discount codes | the **Payment summary** card |
| Pause, cancel, ship now, merge | the title bar |

<Steps>
  <Step title="Find it">
    - In the OpenSubs admin, open **Subscriptions**. Search by subscriber name or email, or paste the subscription number for an exact match.
    - Narrow with the status filter (**All**, **Active**, **Paused**, **Cancelled**, **Failed**, **Billing issues**), or by **Product**, **Frequency**, or **Next charge date**.
    - Starting from a conversation instead? **Subscribers** works too: search the person, open their page, and every subscription they own is listed on it.
    - Click the row to open it.

    <Check>
    **Checkpoint:** the subscription is open, status badge in the title bar.
    </Check>

    <Frame>
      <img src="/images/platform-v2/run/04-subscriptions-list.png" alt="The subscriptions list: search, the status filter, and every subscription one click away." />
    </Frame>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Change the schedule">
    - In the **Schedule** card, pick a new **Next billing date**, a new **Frequency**, or both.
    - Edits collect in the save bar; nothing changes until you hit **Save**.

    <Frame>
      <img src="/images/platform-v2/run/05-subscription-detail.png" alt="Subscription detail: schedule, items, and payment summary on one screen." width="560" />
    </Frame>

    <Note>
    **There is no skip button, on purpose.** Pushing the next billing date one interval forward *is* the skip. Subscribers can skip on their own too, by delaying the next order in their portal or texting SKIP: both covered in [When a subscriber asks for help](/platform-v2/run/when-a-subscriber-asks).
    </Note>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Change the items">
    - In **Items**, edit a line's quantity: quantity edits ride the same save bar as the schedule, so they land in one save.
    - **Add product** opens your catalog: pick the product, set the quantity, and choose whether the line bills as **Subscription** or **One-time**.
    - **Swap** replaces a line with a different product in one move; the remove icon takes a line off after you confirm.
    - Two guards: the last recurring line can't become one-time, and the only line can't be removed. A subscription with nothing recurring on it isn't a subscription.

    <Check>
    **Checkpoint:** you've hit **Save**, and the lines read the way the next order should ship.
    </Check>

    <Frame>
      <img src="/images/platform-v2/run/06-items-editor.png" alt="The items editor: quantities, swap, and add product on the subscription's lines." width="560" />
    </Frame>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Change address, shipping, discounts">
    - The pencil on the **Customer** card opens **Edit shipping address**: pick one of the subscriber's saved addresses with **Use this address**, add one with **Add new address**, or edit a saved one in place.
    - Editing a saved address that other subscriptions ship to warns you at save (**Update N subscriptions?**): saving updates the delivery address on all of them and re-rates their shipping. **Save & update** means all of them.
    - The pencil on the shipping line handles the price: **Set custom price** pins a manual rate; **Use automatic rate** or **Refresh rate** hands it back to your live rates.
    - Discounts live on the **Payment summary** card: type a code into **Discount code**, click **Apply**. Codes stack; remove any one from its chip, or **Remove all** to clear the slate.

    <Note>
    **Custom shipping prices exist only here.** Pinning a price is a merchant move: the portal normally recalculates shipping when a subscriber edits, but a pinned price stays put until you switch back to automatic.
    </Note>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Ship an order now">
    - Click **Ship now** in the title bar. The **Ship an order now** modal lists every line with a checkbox and a quantity; totals update live as you tick.
    - Click **Charge & ship now**. The saved card is charged immediately, the order is created, and the next billing date advances one interval.

    <Warning>
    **No undo.** Once you confirm, the card is charged. And the admin trusts you: unlike the portal, it will run **Ship now** a second time on the same day and bill again.
    </Warning>

    <Check>
    **Checkpoint:** the new order sits in the subscription's orders list, and the next billing date moved one interval out.
    </Check>

    <Frame>
      <img src="/images/platform-v2/run/07-ship-now.png" alt="Ship an order now: per-line checkboxes, live totals, and the Charge & ship now confirm." width="480" />
    </Frame>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Change the status">
    - In the title bar: **Pause** stops billing instantly; **Resume** asks for the next billing date before anything restarts.
    - **Cancel subscription** asks for a cancellation reason (when you've configured them); the cancellation stands in your records even if you later bring the subscription back.
    - Cancelled isn't gone: **Reactivate** starts a cancelled subscription again, next billing date of your choosing.

    <Note>
    **Paused cycles are skipped, never made up.** Nobody gets charged for the paused months: **Resume** asks for the next billing date (it suggests tomorrow) and billing restarts from the date you pick, with nothing owed for the gap. An admin pause is indefinite; only you resume it. (Timed pauses that resume themselves come from the portal and your cancellation flow's pause offer, and those restart on the next date the original rhythm would have hit. While paused, the subscriber's portal shows no next order date at all, even though the admin still does.)
    </Note>

    <Check>
    **Checkpoint:** the status badge in the title bar says what you meant it to.
    </Check>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Merge and split">
    - **Merge subscriptions** appears in the title bar only on an active subscription whose subscriber has other active ones. It opens a picker: the subscriptions you select merge into this one, their recurring items move over, and they stop billing. One-time and gift items stay behind.
    - Merged-away subscriptions end as expired, not cancelled, so they never count as churn. Expired subscriptions are hidden from the list, so don't go hunting for the retired one afterward: the surviving subscription is the record.
    - Going the other way: on an active subscription with two or more recurring lines, **Move to new subscription** on a line splits it onto its own schedule. Same card, same address, same pricing.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read the history">
    - The **History** card is the subscription's diary: every change, day by day, each row expandable, order rows linking to their orders.
    - Every entry names its actor: the subscriber, you, or a quick action link; a change made over SMS is recorded as the subscriber. When someone disputes a change, this is where you settle it.
    - The store-wide version lives at **Analytics > Activity**, with source filters and a CSV export.

    <Check>
    **Checkpoint:** the change in question has a row, and the row says who.
    </Check>
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
**What isn't here, on purpose.** No card entry: the payment-update email handles it, and you never see card numbers ([When a subscriber asks for help](/platform-v2/run/when-a-subscriber-asks)). No manual payment retry: the recovery schedule owns retries ([When a payment fails](/platform-v2/run/when-a-payment-fails)). No delete: cancel is as far as it goes, and the history stays.
</Note>
