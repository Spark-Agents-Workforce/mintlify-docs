Everything in this group is yours to do yourself. That was the point of owning the stack: editing subscriptions, helping subscribers, bulk changes, tuning payment recovery, even updates. None of it waits on a vendor's queue.

The line between your job and getting help sits where the store ends and the code begins:

| Yours to do | Hand to whoever runs your code |
| --- | --- |
| Every recipe in this group: subscriptions, subscribers, bulk changes, recovery tuning, updates. | A code change you want. An update that fails the same way twice. Deploying extension updates (the widget code in your theme and checkout). |

"Whoever runs your code" means the developer or agency who stood up your stack, or [support@opensubs.com](mailto:support@opensubs.com) if no one holds that role. When a page in this group reaches that line, it says so. And the one absolute, worth repeating from [When an alert opens](/platform-v2/run/when-an-alert-opens): never hand-edit the database.

One standing duty: keep three things safe.

- **Your license key.** Proof the platform is yours to run.
- **Your repository access.** The code itself, and the channel your updates arrive through.
- **Your Supabase backups.** Automatic on the Pro plan, with Point-in-Time Recovery. That's your only way back: updates roll forward, never back.

Together they hold your right to run it, your code, your data. Everything else is rebuildable.

## This is the whole job

All of this used to cost a cut of revenue: the billing runs, the retries, the emails, someone watching the queues. Now it runs in accounts you own, and what it leaves you fits in a coffee break. The platform is installed and live, any migration behind you. From here, a few minutes a day. Stuck on your side of the line? Email [support@opensubs.com](mailto:support@opensubs.com) with your store domain and the page you were on.
