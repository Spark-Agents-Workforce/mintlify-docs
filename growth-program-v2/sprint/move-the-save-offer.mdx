Open your cancel flow and count the screens between the cancel click and your save offer. If the reason survey comes first, nobody chose that — it came configured that way, and nobody has audited it since. The audit exists at network scale: across 800k cancel sessions, brands showing an offer *before* the reason step saved 26.6% of cancellations vs 13.8% without (seller) — 500 brands in the pool, and "without" means no offer screen ahead of the survey at all. {/* cid:save-cancellation-flow#008 — canon row 30; basis: 800k sessions, 500 brands */} Per hundred cancel sessions, that is roughly thirteen more logged as saved — in-session, on the deflection basis every dashboard uses (more on that below). {/* cid:save-cancellation-flow#008 — derived: 26.6 − 13.8 ≈ 12.8 per 100 sessions */} Read it as two facts. Most of that gap is having an offer fire before the survey at all. And if your offer already exists but sits after the survey, moving it first is the free version of the same bet.

A subscriber in a cancel flow is walking to the door, and every screen between their click and your offer adds momentum. An offer on the first screen catches them at the coat rack, still deciding. An offer after a three-question survey is chasing them across the parking lot. Now the honest label. The number is Skio's own network, read by Skio, which sells the toggle. {/* cid:save-cancellation-flow#008 (caveat) */} It is correlational, not a randomized test — brands that configure offer screens likely invest more in retention overall. It is also the only screen-order evidence at this scale, and acting on it costs minutes in an editor you already own.

{/* shot:01 keystone-stat "Offer before the reason survey: 26.6% of cancellations saved. Without one: 13.8%." */}

All three major platforms have this screen today. Recharge has a Deflection Page. Skio has a Splash Screen (current builder, February 2026). Stay AI has a Pre-Survey Screen (January 2026). {/* cid:save-cancellation-flow#035, save-cancellation-flow#036, save-cancellation-flow#037 */} That is two owners' worth of judgment rather than three — Recharge bought Skio in April 2026. {/* cid:keep-portal-retention#020, meta-incumbent-playbooks#019 */} Either way: your platform already built the button.

**Time:** 15–40 minutes · **You'll touch:** your subscription platform's cancel-flow editor · **Result shows in:** days

## Do this

{/* shot:02 process-map "The offer moves ahead of the survey — and the cancel button stays visible on every screen." */}

Paths verified against vendor help docs, August 2026 — a desk check, not a live-UI walk. If a menu does not match, search your platform's help for the setting names in bold.

<Warning>
**Keep the cancel button visible.** Under California's ARL (automatic renewal law), any save offer in the cancel flow must sit beside a simultaneously visible cancel button (effective July 1, 2025). {/* cid:meta-category-playbook#015, convert-pdp#023 — canon row 36, acronym glossed */} If you sell into California, this applies to you. If you add or move an offer screen, make sure that a clear cancel path shows on that same screen.
</Warning>

One default before you click: lead with a pause or a delay, not a discount. Offer-first shows the offer to every entrant — including natural abandoners, who leave the flow and stay subscribed at zero cost. A discount pays some of them for doing what they were already doing. A pause does not spend margin.

**You might be thinking:** reason-first is the responsible order — you match the offer to the reason. Two answers. First, the survey still runs, after the offer instead of before it. On Recharge, subscribers who accept bypass the survey. Decliners still answer it. You lose reasons only from the people the dashboard logs as saved. Second, reason-matching is not where the save comes from. In the only controlled test, optimized reason-agnostic offers roughly doubled the save rate vs reason-matched routing (seller). {/* cid:save-cancellation-flow#029 — canon row 34, rewritten per R2 brief; row amendment proposed */} The base: one brand — Vital Proteins — whose deflect rate went from 17% to 23% over a year. Session counts were not published. Reason data diagnoses. It does not have to gate the offer. (The same momentum model predicts a shorter survey lifts saves too — nobody has published that test either.)

## You'll see

| Platform | Read the save rate at |
| --- | --- |
| Recharge | Churn tools > Cancellation prevention > your flow > **⋮ More actions > Reports**, Performance tab |
| Skio | **Analytics > Cancel Flow Dashboard**, the "Cancel flow performance" block — updates every 4 hours {/* cid:numbers-kpi-dashboard#003 */} |
| Stay AI | **Analytics > Cancel Survey**, "Save Rate Through Cancel Survey" |

You already recorded the before side in the baseline step — a window that covers about 900 entrants, as far back as the definition holds. After the change, read the same screen over the next ~900. The entrant count lives on that same screen — Recharge calls it **Experiences delivered**, and Skio and Stay count cancel-flow sessions. Compare only numbers from the same definition.

<Note>
Platform save rates count survey abandoners as saved — treat every dashboard save rate as a deflection metric, never a retention outcome. {/* cid:save-churn-diagnostics#019, numbers-kpi-dashboard#015, numbers-kpi-dashboard#016 — canon row 26 */} Roughly a quarter of cancel-flow entrants deflect in-session but only ~17% are still saved at 30 days — always quote both numbers (seller). {/* cid:save-cancellation-flow#005 — canon row 27 */} That pair is Chargebee's 2022 e-commerce benchmark. Your before/after still works, because the definition holds constant. On Stay AI it does not hold across the 2026 survey switch — never compare a new-survey save rate to a RetentionEngine-era one. {/* cid:numbers-kpi-dashboard#016 */}
</Note>

To read a 5-point move on a 15% base, you need about 905 entrants on each side of the change. {/* cid:run-operating-cadence#010 */} If 300 subscribers enter your cancel flow each month, that is roughly three months per side. Do not judge the result at week one — the dashboard moves daily, the verdict does not.

One named case, for calibration: Skio's Cornbread Hemp went from a 9.5% save rate to 25.6% (no session counts published) — after adding a splash screen and reworking its offers in the same push, so read it as a vendor-published ceiling, not a promise. {/* cid:save-cancellation-flow#009 */}

**Checkpoint:** the save rate rises on the same definition, and the offer screen shows a visible cancel path.
