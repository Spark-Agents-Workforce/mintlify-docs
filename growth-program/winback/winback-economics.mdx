A reactivation or resume is the first action. The economic result is what the return produces after costs and whether paid orders continue over time. This lesson helps you follow each return path through paid orders, direct costs, and later subscriber behavior. The result is one decision: keep, change, expand, or stop the path.

<div className="os-pull not-prose">
  <p>Count the paid return. Then count whether it lasts.</p>
</div>

## What this lesson gives you

- A way to compare cancelled-subscription reactivation and paused-subscription resumption without combining their results.
- A staged review from the eligible group through the first action, paid orders, later health, and contribution after costs.
- A return-path scorecard with a diagnosis, a decision, an owner, and a next review.

## Keep each return path separate

[Reactivating cancelled](/growth-program/winback/reactivating-cancelled) and [Resuming paused](/growth-program/winback/reactivating-paused) define who enters each path, what the subscriber sees, and when messages stop. Bring those plans here. Do not combine their results.

| Return path | First action | First paid outcome | Later health read |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cancelled: voluntary cancellation** | The cancelled subscription reactivates | A paid order completes after reactivation | A later paid order, another cancellation, and whether the same reason returns |
| **Cancelled: payment recovery ended** | The cancelled subscription reactivates after recovery has ended | Payment succeeds and a paid order completes | A later paid renewal, another cancellation after payment recovery ends, or a voluntary cancellation |
| **Cancelled: cancelled from pause** | The subscription that was paused and then cancelled reactivates | A paid order completes after reactivation | A later paid order, another appropriate pause, or another cancellation |
| **Paused: defined duration** | The subscription resumes automatically on its scheduled date or earlier after the subscriber acts | The next planned paid order completes | A later paid order, an extension or another appropriate pause, or cancellation |
| **Paused: open-ended** | The subscription resumes after the subscriber chooses to return | The next planned paid order completes | A later paid order, another appropriate pause, or cancellation |

These paths start from different subscriber decisions. A voluntary cancellation may require a changed product, cadence, or offer. A subscription that ended after payment recovery needs a working payment path. A subscription cancelled from pause needs its own reactivation read because the subscriber moved from a temporary stop to a full cancellation. Their raw return rates are not directly comparable.

Review defined-duration and open-ended pauses separately. A defined-duration pause already has a scheduled resume, so distinguish automatic resumes from early resumes after a message. An open-ended pause has no automatic resume date. Remaining paused can still be the right outcome when the subscriber is not ready to return.

## Measure the return in stages

Use the eligible group at path entry as the main denominator. Keep the definition stable through the review window.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Record the eligible group">
    Count the subscriptions that met the path's entry rules before the return sequence or scheduled resume. Keep voluntary cancellations, payment-recovery-ended cancellations, cancelled-from-pause subscriptions, defined-duration pauses, and open-ended pauses in separate groups.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Record the first action">
    Count reactivations for cancelled subscriptions and resumes for paused subscriptions. For defined-duration pauses, separate automatic resumes from early resumes after a message. This records the first action, not paid revenue.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm the first paid order">
    Count a paid return only after the first paid order completes. A click or payment-information update is not a completed paid order.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read the later outcome">
    Use the same review window for everyone in the group. Count later paid orders, repeat cancellation, the cancellation reason when available, and the final subscription state.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add costs and make the decision">
    Calculate contribution after return costs, identify the weakest stage, then decide whether to keep, change, expand, or stop the path.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Useful rates are:

| Rate | Calculation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Immediate action rate** | Reactivated cancelled subscriptions or resumed paused subscriptions ÷ eligible subscriptions in that path |
| **Paid return rate** | Subscriptions with a first paid order ÷ eligible subscriptions |
| **Later paid outcome rate** | Subscriptions with the defined later paid outcome ÷ eligible subscriptions |
| **Later-order rate** | Subscriptions with a later paid order ÷ subscriptions with a first paid order |
| **Repeat-ending rate** | Subscriptions with a first paid order that later end again ÷ subscriptions with a first paid order |

Define the later paid outcome before reviewing the group. It should be a later completed order at a product-appropriate checkpoint, measured the same way for everyone in that path.

Choose a review window that gives the returned subscriber a fair chance to reach the later checkpoint. A daily consumable, a longer-cadence wellness product, and an occasional-use product should not inherit one universal window. Mark a group incomplete until every included subscription has had the full review window. Do not compare an incomplete group with a completed one as if both were final.

## Count what the return produced and cost

[Subscription economics](/growth-program/numbers/subscription-economics) owns the contribution-margin definition. Use the same definition here so the return path can be compared with other growth work.

| Calculation step | What to include |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Start with gross returned revenue** | Sales from completed paid orders in the return group before direct costs. This shows volume, not profit. |
| **Subtract revenue reductions** | Discounts, redeemed credits, refunds, and returns when they are not already removed from the revenue figure. |
| **Subtract direct order costs** | Product cost, fulfillment, merchant-funded shipping, and payment costs. |
| **Subtract return-path costs** | Gifts, SMS, paid media, direct mail, and extra support work when those costs are material to the decision. |
| **Result: return-path contribution** | What remains after the reductions and direct costs above. |

Avoid counting a cost twice. If your sales report already shows revenue after discounts, credits, refunds, or returns, do not subtract them again. If you use gross margin percentage to account for product cost, do not subtract product cost again.

Calculate **contribution per eligible subscription** by dividing return-path contribution by the eligible subscriptions in that path. Use the full eligible group, not only subscribers who returned, so the cost of non-responders remains visible.

Use realized paid orders through a common window before relying on a long-range forecast. If you project later value, build the projection from mature returned groups and label it as a projection.

## Find the weak stage

Review each return path and message version first. Split the result by cancellation reason when available, pause model, offer, product, or cadence only when the split changes what you will do next.

| What looks weak | What to review next | Where the change belongs |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **First action** | Message relevance, timing, and whether the return action is clear and available | Update the message, timing, or direct return path in Reactivating cancelled or Resuming paused |
| **First paid order** | Payment path, next order timing, returned subscription setup, and the choices shown before confirmation | Fix the payment or return setup and give the subscriber a clearer product, cadence, quantity, or next-order choice |
| **Later health** | Later paid orders, repeated cancellation, another pause, and the original cancellation problem | Improve the product, cadence, return experience, or the upstream problem identified in Reading your churn |
| **Contribution** | Incentive cost, merchant-funded shipping, channel cost, direct mail, and material support work | Narrow the audience, reduce the cost, change the offer, or stop the added spend |

Stop adding report cuts when another cut would not change the decision. Small groups can create dramatic rates that do not hold up when more subscriptions reach the full review window.

## Know what your platform report tells you

Klaviyo, your subscription platform, or another reporting tool may show reactivations, resumes, and revenue after a message under its reporting rules. These platform-attributed results are useful for monitoring a path and comparing versions under the same rules.

The report does not always show what the message alone caused. This matters most when a subscriber could have returned without added outreach, such as a scheduled resume. Without a credible comparison, describe the result as observed or platform-attributed, not incremental.

## Decide where effort and spend belong

Start with the lowest-cost relevant path that gives the subscriber a clear way to return. Email often has a low direct send cost, but it still uses subscriber attention and can create unsubscribe pressure. SMS, paid media, direct mail, incentives, and manual support add costs that must earn their place.

| Decision | Use it when |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Keep** | The path produces paid orders and positive contribution, later behavior is acceptable, and the subscriber experience remains clear. |
| **Change** | Subscribers reactivate or resume but do not complete a paid order, return into the wrong setup, or end again for the same unresolved reason. |
| **Expand** | The path produces positive contribution, later outcomes remain healthy, and the wider audience still fits. Use a formal comparison before materially increasing spend or incentive cost when you need to know what the added effort changed. |
| **Stop** | The path is no longer relevant, costs exceed contribution, repeated endings show the original problem remains, or subscriber pressure is too high. |

Do not use one path's economics to justify another. A voluntary-cancellation offer does not set the budget for payment-recovery-ended subscribers. A cancelled-from-pause reactivation does not belong in the ordinary voluntary-cancellation group. A scheduled pause does not prove that a reminder caused the resume. Review each path on its own job.

No universal contribution target clears every path. Set the threshold from your own costs, margin requirements, and the decision at stake.

## The artifact: the return-path scorecard

Complete one scorecard for each separate return path and message version. Do not combine cancelled and paused subscriptions, defined-duration and open-ended pauses, or completed and incomplete review groups. Split the scorecard further only when the split changes what you will do.

| Scorecard field | What to record |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Path and version** | Cancelled or paused; the specific return path or pause model; the message or campaign version |
| **Review group** | Entry period, review-through date, and whether the group is incomplete or complete |
| **Eligible subscriptions** | The subscriptions that met the path's entry rules before the sequence or scheduled resume |
| **First actions** | Reactivated cancelled subscriptions or resumed paused subscriptions; for defined-duration pauses, automatic and early resumes separately |
| **Paid outcomes** | Subscriptions with a first paid order, a later paid order, and a repeat ending |
| **Revenue and costs** | Gross returned revenue, reductions not already reflected, direct order costs, and material return-path costs |
| **Experience signals** | Unsubscribes, complaints, support load, or another signal that the path is creating pressure |
| **Calculated results** | Immediate action rate, paid return rate, later paid outcome rate, later-order rate, repeat-ending rate, total contribution, and contribution per eligible subscription |
| **Diagnosis** | The weakest stage and the message, timing, setup, product, cadence, experience, or cost that needs review |
| **Decision** | Keep, change, expand, or stop, with one sentence explaining why |
| **Owner and next review** | The person responsible and the next completed period or review date |

Save the scorecard beside the return plans from the first two M9 lessons. Bring the decision and next review into M10's operating calendar. Return repeated cancellation reasons or poor product and cadence fit to [Reading your churn](/growth-program/churn-defense/reading-your-churn) instead of trying to solve them with more messages.
