A paused subscriber has not cancelled. They have asked for time.

Your job is to help them resume when the timing fits, make another change when it does not, and stop asking when their situation changes. That calls for a different plan from the one you use for a cancelled subscriber.

## What this lesson gives you

- The order of changes to offer before a subscriber pauses.
- A way to separate defined, extended, and open-ended pauses.
- A practical resume message plan for each pause situation.
- Clear actions, stop conditions, and outcomes to review.

## Try targeted changes before pausing

A pause should come after the more targeted flexibility options. Before the subscriber confirms a pause, make these choices easy to find in this order:

1. **Change the next order date** when the subscriber knows when they want the next delivery.
2. **Skip the upcoming order** when they only need to miss one shipment.
3. **Change delivery frequency** when orders repeatedly arrive too soon.
4. **Pause the subscription** when those changes do not give enough room.

If a pause is still the right choice, the subscriber can choose a defined duration or leave the pause open-ended. [Flexibility as retention](/growth-program/account-area/flexibility-as-retention) covers how these choices prevent an avoidable cancellation, and [Cancellation flow design](/growth-program/churn-defense/cancellation-flow-design) covers where they fit before cancellation.

## Start with the pause situation

Read the current situation before you send anything. The same subscriber may move through several of these situations, so the message should always match what is true now.

| Pause situation | What it means | What you do next |
|---|---|---|
| **Defined pause** | The subscriber chooses a pause duration, which creates a planned resume date. | Confirm the duration and date, then remind the subscriber before the subscription resumes. |
| **Extended pause** | The subscriber needs more time and chooses a later resume date. | Confirm the new date and replace any reminders tied to the old date. |
| **Open-ended pause** | There is no planned resume date. | Use a short, limited check-in sequence that helps the subscriber resume, choose a date, or remain paused. |
| **Resumed subscription** | The subscription is active again. | Stop pause messages and move the subscriber to the normal active-subscriber experience. |
| **Cancelled from pause** | The subscriber ended the subscription while it was paused. | Stop pause messages and move the subscription to a distinct cancelled-from-pause reactivation path. |

The message should always match the current situation. A reminder tied to an old resume date should not survive an extension, resumption, or cancellation.

## Use the context the pause gives you

A pause normally does not ask the subscriber to choose a reason. Do not make a pause reason a core input, and do not guess why someone paused.

Build the resume plan from the information the subscriber has already given you through their choices:

- Whether the pause is defined or open-ended.
- The selected duration and planned resume date, when one exists.
- The product and current delivery frequency.
- What the subscriber actually does, such as extending the pause, choosing a date, resuming, or cancelling.

For health and wellness products, a short message can reinforce accurate product-use guidance, realistic consistency expectations, or relevant education. Keep it useful and brief. Do not imply results or progress you cannot see, and do not turn a resume reminder into a long sales pitch.

Cancellation is different. If a subscriber cancels while paused, the cancellation reason can help shape the later reactivation message. That is cancellation context, not a pause reason.

## Build the messages around the next decision

There is no perfect pause calendar. For a defined pause, work back from the planned resume date so the subscriber has time to anticipate the order and make another change. For an open-ended pause, choose a review point that makes sense for the product and normal usage. Then review your own outcomes and adjust.

| Message path | When to send | Message job | Main action | Possible channel | Stop when |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Defined pause: confirmation** | When the pause begins | Confirm the selected duration and planned resume date. | Review the paused subscription | Email | The date changes, the subscription resumes, or it is cancelled. |
| **Defined pause: resume reminder** | Before the planned resume date, with enough time to act | Show what will resume and when. Make another change easy. | Review, resume now, or choose a later date | Email, with a short SMS reminder if useful | The subscriber takes an action or the planned resume date passes. |
| **Extended pause** | When the resume date changes | Confirm the new date and set the right expectation. | Review the updated pause | Email | The new date is confirmed. Retire the old reminder schedule. |
| **Open-ended pause: check-in** | At the review point you chose | Ask whether the subscriber is ready to resume or wants to pick a date. | Resume, choose a resume date, or remain paused | Email | The subscriber resumes, chooses a date, or cancels. |
| **Open-ended pause: final check-in** | Later, if the first check-in received no action | Leave a final useful path back without pressure. | Review the paused subscription | Email or a short SMS | The subscriber acts, or the sequence reaches its planned end. |
| **Cancelled from pause: reactivation** | After cancellation, on the timing set for this distinct path | Acknowledge that the subscription was paused and is now cancelled. Use the cancellation reason when it changes the message. | Review reactivation options | Email | The subscriber reactivates or the reactivation sequence ends. |

The sequence is a starting point, not a rule about exact timing. A product used daily may need a different lead time from one used occasionally. A defined pause gives you a date to work from, while an open-ended pause does not. Use the information you already have, then test the timing against your own completed pause periods.

## Make resuming easy

Every resume message should lead to the paused subscription, not a generic store page. The subscriber should be able to see what will resume, when the next order is planned, and what they can still change.

Before you launch the sequence, check that the path lets the subscriber:

- Review the product, delivery frequency, and next order date.
- Resume the subscription or choose a resume date without searching for the right control.
- Make a relevant change before confirming the resumption.
- See a clear confirmation after the action is complete.

If the subscriber still needs room, help them extend the pause or adjust the schedule. A discount does not solve a timing problem.

## Stop messages when they no longer fit

Pause messages should end as soon as the subscriber's situation changes.

- **Resumed:** stop the pause sequence and move to active-subscriber messages.
- **Extended or rescheduled:** stop reminders tied to the old date and use the new one.
- **Cancelled from pause:** stop the pause sequence and move the subscription to the distinct cancelled-from-pause reactivation path.
- **Still paused after the final check-in:** stop the sequence. Do not turn an open-ended pause into an endless reminder loop.

These stops matter as much as the messages. A reminder about an old date tells the subscriber you were not paying attention.

The cancelled-from-pause reactivation message can build on the context used while the subscription was paused, but it must say that the subscription is now cancelled. It can also use the cancellation reason. Do not send a generic cancellation winback that ignores the paused history. [Reactivating cancelled](/growth-program/winback/recapturing-cancelled) covers the wider reactivation sequence.

## Review what happened

Review completed pause periods, not clicks alone. For each path, look at how many subscribers:

- Resumed.
- Chose a later resume date.
- Remained paused.
- Cancelled from pause.
- Completed the next planned order after resuming.

Then compare the result with the pause type, selected duration, planned resume date, message timing, product, delivery frequency, and the subscriber's actual actions. If one path repeatedly produces extensions, the timing may be too early or the original delivery frequency may still be wrong. If subscribers resume and then cancel around the next order, the resume experience may not be setting the right expectation.

Use your own completed periods as the baseline. The goal is not to force every pause into a quick resumption. It is to help the subscriber resume at a time that fits and reduce avoidable cancellations caused by surprise or poor timing.

## The artifact: the pause-state sequence plan

Fill in one row for each pause situation you support. Keep the message job and main action narrow enough that a subscriber can understand both at a glance.

| Pause situation | Timing you will use | Message job | Main action | Stop condition | Outcome to review |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Defined pause** | *At pause start; before the planned resume: ______* | Confirm what resumes and when | Review, resume, or change the date | Date changes, resume, or cancel | Resume, extension, cancel, next order completed |
| **Extended pause** | *When the resume date changes: ______* | Confirm the new date | Review the updated pause | New date confirmed | Later resume or another extension |
| **Open-ended pause** | *First review point: ______* | Ask for the next decision | Resume, choose a date, or remain paused | Action or final check-in | Resume, defined pause, remain paused, or cancel |
| **Resumed subscription** | *Immediately after resumption* | Confirm what happens next | Review the next order | Resumption confirmed | Next order completed or changed |
| **Cancelled from pause** | *After cancellation: ______* | Confirm the subscription is cancelled and offer the relevant reactivation path | Review reactivation options | Reactivation or sequence end | Reactivation, no action, or later reactivation |

Use this plan to set up the sequence in the tools you already use. Read it again after a completed review period, change the rows that are not helping, and leave the paths that are working alone.
