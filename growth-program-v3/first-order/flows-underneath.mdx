Bone-dry on purpose: this is the plumbing that makes the four stages run unattended, and the one number that grades them.

## The scoreboard

<Steps>
  <Step title="The figure">
    One number grades this bucket: **the percentage of new subscribers who hit the activation point within 30 days.** Beneath it, the flow benchmarks that tell you whether the machinery itself is healthy: 50-65% open, 5-12% click, 5-15% review collection, 8-20% repeat purchase from the post-purchase flow.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The fix">
    If the activation number is low, the playbook's prescription is blunt: make onboarding simpler, more personal, or more guided — in that order of investigation. If the flow benchmarks are low while activation holds, the problem is the sends, not the program.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The why">
    Usage churn precedes billing churn — the subscriber who never activated in month one is the cancel in month two. The 30-day number is the earliest leading indicator the bucket produces, which is why it, and not revenue, is the scoreboard.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The journey spine

The corpus's four milestones, in order: **Activate, Testimonial, Refer, Ascend** — each with its unlock (the next thing opens at Activate, spotlight and badge at Testimonial, a free month or credit at Refer, VIP tier or founder pricing at Ascend). This bucket owns the first milestone; the flow hands a subscriber to [First weeks](/growth-program-v3/first-weeks) carrying the other three. Build it as an ordered sequence — the corpus's eight-stage journey pattern — so every subscriber is always in exactly one place with one next milestone.

## Flow hygiene

- **The split.** The flow branches first-time versus repeat buyer at entry — a repeat buyer gets loyalty acknowledged and brand education skipped. First-order treatment is for first orders.
- **Auto-exit.** Buyers leave prospect flows the moment they buy (the has-not-ordered-since-entering filter). Nothing marks a machine like a welcome-series discount arriving after the purchase.
- **Spacing.** Automated sends respect a 16-hour minimum gap, and the overload ceiling is corpus law: do not bury the subscriber in automation.
- **Send windows.** Align to observed peak trading hours — send when your buyers demonstrably shop.
- **Coupons.** Unique per-subscriber codes generated by the store integration, never static codes that leak to coupon sites.

## Personalization laws

Real account context only — name, milestone, what they bought, account-health signals. Broken personalization is worse than none; "Hey there, %name!" is the corpus's own cautionary artifact. And the context has to arrive: buyer behavior history moves from acquisition to retention **at the moment of signup** — what they browsed, which claim converted them — so the ritual emails speak to the person, not the segment.

## Status plumbing

The billing corpus's states, because the flows must know them: a subscription with an immediate payment due sits in **incomplete** until the first invoice lands; on automatic collection there is a **23-hour window** to pay it; past that it is **incomplete_expired** — the order that never became a customer. Watch that state and alert on it: it is the only churn that happens before the relationship starts, and it is invisible in every revenue report because the revenue never existed.
