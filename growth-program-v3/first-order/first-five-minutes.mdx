The order exists; the relationship does not yet. Three surfaces fire in the first five minutes — the screen, the receipt, the welcome email — and one capture belongs here because it never gets easier: the second card. This chapter is the confirmation stage end to end.

## The figure

Post-purchase messages are the best-read email your brand will ever send — the flow benchmarks run 50-65% open rates, because the recipient is actively awaiting order information. Whatever rides next to that information gets the highest attention you will ever have for free. The first five minutes spend it.

## The thank-you page

<Info>
**Frontier: OpenSubs-original territory.** The screen one second after payment is the highest-attention surface in the whole subscription lifecycle — and across all 773 mined cards there is not one about it. The corpus wrote the pre-purchase page a dozen laws and the post-purchase screen zero. What transfers: the one-next-step law from the welcome email below, and the five-question skeleton from [Sell the offer](/growth-program-v3/get-in/sell-the-offer). What OpenSubs is writing first, from scratch: the thank-you page as activation surface — what arrives when, the one thing to do before the box lands, and the reply channel opened on screen. Under development; no borrowed numbers to lean on, and none faked.
</Info>

Until that play ships, run the transfers: one primary next step on the page (not a feature tour), the ship window and first-renewal date in plain text, and a human way to reply.

## The welcome email

<Steps>
  <Step title="The figure">
    The lifecycle-email corpus is specific about the first send: it is the thank-you plus what-happens-next, immediately on signup — and the retention playbook's follow-up law paces what comes after: first follow-up inside 24-48 hours, and the subscriber always knows when they will hear from you next.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The fix">
    Four laws from the email-template corpus, verbatim in spirit: name ONE primary next step, not a feature list. State the dates in plain text — what ships when, when the first renewal charges. Close with a P.S. that invites a reply. And carry a fallback plain URL for when the button fails — on every lifecycle email, not just this one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The why">
    A reply channel opened on day zero is a complaint channel that skips the public review — and the complaint clock matters: a low-tenure customer with an active complaint is 3-4x more likely to churn, so the cheapest possible complaint surface belongs in the first email. The one-next-step law exists because a new subscriber given five things to do does none of them.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The receipt

<Steps>
  <Step title="The figure">
    The receipt-template corpus treats the receipt as three plays wearing a legal document. It is also transactional email — meaning it rides the same 50-65% open class as the rest of the confirmation stage.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The fix">
    Play one: a next-purchase coupon with an expiry date — a second sale riding a transactional open. Play two: the statement descriptor and card last-four named in plain text — "this will appear as X on your statement." Play three: a proactive card-update prompt keyed to life events — moved recently, new card — linked twice, inline and footer.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The why">
    The descriptor line is chargeback insurance: the subscriber who recognizes the charge does not dispute it. The card-update prompt is the cheapest dunning email you will ever send, because it goes out before anything fails. Both are churn plays disguised as bookkeeping.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The second card

<Steps>
  <Step title="The figure">
    The pricing playbook's basis: roughly 1.2-1.7% of subscribers a month are lost to card-information changes alone — at a 5% total monthly churn rate, that is 24-34% of everything you lose. Its worked math on the fix: a second payment method on file is worth +31% to +51% LTV on a \$100-a-month service, against the +4% the fee itself collects.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The fix">
    Capture the backup card at the sale — checkout or the day-zero email — framed as never-miss-a-box. The playbook's own script sells it with the honest number: about 7% of cards decline every month; the backup card is how your deliveries ignore that. [The crack](/growth-program-v3/the-crack) teaches the recovery machinery that uses it; this chapter's job is only the capture, at the moment commitment is highest and friction lowest.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The why">
    Fee revenue is linear; churn reduction compounds through the lifetime denominator. And the capture window is real: a subscriber five minutes past a yes adds a card in one click. The same ask in month three is a security interrogation.
  </Step>
</Steps>
