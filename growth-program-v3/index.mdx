You run a CPG subscription brand on Shopify. And you know the difference between running a store and growing one.

A subscription business is a track your customers roll down. Growth is four jobs: put people on the track, stop them jumping off, patch the cracks they fall through, and make every customer bigger as they roll.

Everything in this program lives in one of seven buckets. And one pair of hands — support — works all seven.

<Note>
Come back sits in the middle of the machine, not at the end. The return track catches people who jumped out or fell through the crack, loops them around, and merges them back on **before** the growth run. A won-back subscriber re-enters the journey — and then gets bigger.
</Note>

## The hands work all seven buckets

Support is not an eighth bucket, because it is not a place customers go. It is the pair of hands working every bucket you just read.

Most stores get this backwards: they shrink support to zero and call every ticket a failure. Wrong. A ticket is a customer **volunteering** a direct conversation — the thing brands pay ads, quizzes, and surveys to fake.

With AI you do not choose between cheap and good. Machines kill the forced tickets and answer the boring questions — that is the cost line. Your humans, with AI at their side, work the conversations that matter — that is the marketing channel.

Two jobs, every bucket: patch what is broken, and leave them with a little more than they asked for. The hands do not just stop the leaks. The hands grow the customers.

## How every play in this program is written

Every play page answers three things, in order:

<Steps>
  <Step title="The figure">
    The number that tells you whether this is your problem, and how big.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The fix">
    The exact play — what to change, where to click, what to send.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The why">
    The mechanism, so you can adapt the play when your store is the exception.
  </Step>
</Steps>

That is the promise: the figure, the fix, and the page that says why.
